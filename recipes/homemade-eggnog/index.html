Homemade Eggnog

A silky, gently spiced homemade eggnog made with a cooked custard base, finished with cream and plenty of fresh nutmeg.

KITCHEN NOTE

Kitchen note: Use an instant-read thermometer for the cooked custard. A common setback is Boiling the custard can cause it to split.

Published 2026-11-11

⏱ 2 hr 30 min🍽 6 servings🔥 245 cal
DessertUSAChristmas
↓ Jump to recipe

Ingredients

  • 4 large egg yolks
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Freshly grated nutmeg, for serving

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Whisk egg yolks and sugar in a heatproof bowl until the mixture turns pale and thick enough to leave a ribbon for a second.
  2. Step 2.Warm milk, cream, cinnamon and nutmeg in a saucepan until steaming but not boiling, then remove the cinnamon stick before tempering the yolks.
  3. Step 3.Slowly whisk a ladle of warm milk into the yolks, then repeat once more; this gradual step keeps the custard smooth instead of scrambling the eggs.
  4. Step 4.Pour the tempered mixture back into the pan and cook over low heat, stirring constantly, until it lightly coats a spoon and reaches 160°F.
  5. Step 5.Strain into a jug, stir in vanilla, and cool to room temperature before chilling for at least two hours so the spices settle into the custard.
  6. Step 6.Serve cold with freshly grated nutmeg; for the creamiest texture, stir the chilled eggnog well just before pouring.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Use an instant-read thermometer for the cooked custard.

! Common Mistakes

  • Boiling the custard can cause it to split.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use half-and-half in place of the milk and cream.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate covered for up to 3 days.
