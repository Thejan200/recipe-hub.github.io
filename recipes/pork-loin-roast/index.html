Pork Loin Roast

Herb-rubbed centre-cut pork loin roasted over onions, checked by temperature, and rested before slicing for a moist family-style roast.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Pork loin is much larger than tenderloin and requires its own timing. Keep this in mind too: Over-roasting is the usual reason a pork loin becomes dry.

Ingredients

  • 3 1/2 lb boneless center-cut pork loin roast
  • 1 1/2 tbsp olive oil
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tsp chopped rosemary
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 large onion, thickly sliced

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Heat the oven to 350°F. While it warms, let the loin sit briefly and prepare the herb coating.
  2. Step 2.Dry the roast well. Trim away only thick, loose fat and leave a thin layer to protect the surface.
  3. Step 3.Mix olive oil, salt, pepper, rosemary, thyme, garlic powder, onion powder, and Dijon into a spreadable paste. Cover the pork evenly.
  4. Step 4.Make a bed of sliced onion in a roasting pan, set the pork fat-side up over it, and pour broth into the pan without washing the coating from the roast.
  5. Step 5.Roast uncovered, beginning temperature checks after about 50 minutes.
  6. Step 6.Remove the pork when the thickest central area reaches 145°F; this is more reliable than a fixed roasting time.
  7. Step 7.Move the roast to a board and loosely tent it. Keep the roasting pan for its juices.
  8. Step 8.Rest for at least 15 minutes before cutting so the meat does not lose its juices immediately.
  9. Step 9.If desired, skim the pan liquid and simmer it briefly, scraping up the browned onion and roasting bits.
  10. Step 10.Cut the loin across the grain into even slices and serve with the onions and warm pan liquid.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Pork loin is much larger than tenderloin and requires its own timing.
  • Use a thermometer so a lean roast is not taken too far.
  • A full resting period makes cleaner, juicier slices.

! Common Mistakes

  • Over-roasting is the usual reason a pork loin becomes dry.
  • Broth poured directly over the top can strip off the herb coating.
  • Cutting immediately after roasting lets too much juice run onto the board.

↔ Substitutions

  • Sage can replace rosemary.
  • Vegetable broth can replace chicken broth.
  • Whole-grain mustard gives the coating a coarser texture than Dijon.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate sliced leftovers for up to four days.
  • Reheat covered with a small amount of broth or pan juices.
  • Freeze tightly wrapped cooled slices for up to two months.
