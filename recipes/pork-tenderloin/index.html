Pork Tenderloin

A quick skillet-to-oven pork tenderloin with an aromatic herb crust, browned exterior, and a temperature-guided juicy centre.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Silver skin stays chewy after cooking, so remove it before seasoning. Keep this in mind too: Pork loin and tenderloin are different cuts and do not share a cooking time.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb pork tenderloin, silver skin removed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 tbsp avocado or olive oil
  • 1 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 2 garlic cloves, lightly crushed

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Heat the oven to 400°F. Slide a small sharp knife just under the silver skin and remove it without taking away much of the lean meat.
  2. Step 2.Blot the tenderloin dry. Combine the salt, pepper, Italian seasoning, garlic powder, and coriander in a small bowl.
  3. Step 3.Rub the pork with half of the oil, then coat it evenly with the seasoning mixture.
  4. Step 4.Warm an oven-safe skillet over medium-high heat with the remaining oil. Add the tenderloin only once the oil is shimmering.
  5. Step 5.Turn the pork until the surface has browned on all sides; this step develops flavour but does not finish the centre.
  6. Step 6.Add the butter and crushed garlic. When the butter foams, spoon it over the pork for a moment before moving the skillet to the oven.
  7. Step 7.Roast until an instant-read thermometer in the thickest section reads 145°F. Start checking early because tenderloins vary in thickness.
  8. Step 8.Transfer the meat to a board and loosely cover it, keeping any juices from the pan.
  9. Step 9.Let it rest for 8 to 10 minutes so the centre settles before cutting.
  10. Step 10.Slice across the grain into medallions and spoon over the resting juices before serving.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Silver skin stays chewy after cooking, so remove it before seasoning.
  • An instant-read thermometer is more reliable than timing alone for this lean cut.
  • Resting is part of the cooking process and helps keep the slices moist.

! Common Mistakes

  • Pork loin and tenderloin are different cuts and do not share a cooking time.
  • Putting damp pork into a hot skillet prevents a good brown crust.
  • Cooking well beyond 145°F can leave the centre dry.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use dried rosemary and thyme in place of Italian seasoning.
  • A neutral vegetable oil works in place of avocado oil.
  • For a sharper coating, spread Dijon mustard under the spice blend.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate cooled sliced pork for up to three days.
  • Reheat gently with a small splash of stock or pan juices.
  • Freeze cooled slices in a sealed container for up to two months.
