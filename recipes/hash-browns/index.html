Hash Browns

Skillet hash browns made from well-dried russet potato shreds, cooked in a thin layer for a crackly crust and a soft potato middle.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/4 lb russet potatoes
  • 1/4 tsp onion powder
  • 2 tbsp olive oil or bacon fat
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Scrub or peel the potatoes, then shred them with the coarse side of a box grater.
  2. Step 2.Pile the shreds into a clean tea towel and twist hard over the sink until the towel is no longer releasing much liquid.
  3. Step 3.Loosen the potato strands in a bowl and distribute the onion powder through them without compressing the mixture.
  4. Step 4.Warm a heavy 10-inch skillet over medium heat. Add half of the oil and butter and wait until the butter stops foaming vigorously.
  5. Step 5.Scatter in half of the potatoes and gently level them into a loose, thin round; leave them alone rather than pressing repeatedly.
  6. Step 6.Let the underside brown for 5 to 6 minutes, adjusting the heat down if the butter darkens before the potatoes colour.
  7. Step 7.Season the exposed side, then turn the round with a broad spatula, or turn it in manageable wedges if that is easier.
  8. Step 8.Drizzle the remaining cooking fat around the edge and brown the second side until it feels firm and crisp.
  9. Step 9.Move the finished hash browns to a warm plate or rack, then cook the second batch in the same way.
  10. Step 10.Add a final pinch of seasoning and bring them to the table immediately.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Dry potato shreds thoroughly before they meet the pan; surface water prevents browning.
  • A heavy pan holds its heat better when the cold potatoes are added.
  • Cook in two thin rounds instead of making one thick layer.

! Common Mistakes

  • Leaving water in the shreds gives the potatoes a steamed, pale finish.
  • Moving the potatoes before a crust forms makes the round difficult to turn.
  • Using high heat throughout can scorch the butter while the centre remains undercooked.

↔ Substitutions

  • Rendered bacon fat can replace the olive oil for a savoury, smoky note.
  • For a mild onion flavour, fold in a little grated onion after squeezing it dry.
  • Ghee is a useful alternative to butter when a slightly higher cooking temperature is needed.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Cool leftovers promptly and keep them covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.
  • Restore the crust in a dry skillet, oven, or air fryer rather than microwaving.
  • Freeze fully cooled portions in a single layer, then transfer them to a freezer-safe container for up to 1 month.
