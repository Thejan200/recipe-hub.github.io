Eggs Benedict

Eggs Benedict assembled from toasted English muffins, warmed Canadian bacon, softly poached eggs, and a lemony butter hollandaise.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Keep poaching water at a quiet simmer. Keep this in mind too: Rapidly boiling water can tear delicate whites.

Ingredients

  • 2 English muffins, split
  • 4 slices Canadian bacon
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 tbsp white vinegar
  • 3 egg yolks
  • 10 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1/4 tsp kosher salt
  • Pinch of cayenne
  • Chopped chives, for garnish

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Toast the muffin halves until golden and keep them warm while the other parts are prepared.
  2. Step 2.Warm the Canadian bacon in a skillet until lightly browned, then put a slice on each muffin half.
  3. Step 3.Bring a wide pan of water to a quiet simmer and add vinegar; a hard boil can break up the whites.
  4. Step 4.Crack each egg into a small cup, ease it into the water, and poach until the white is set around a soft yolk.
  5. Step 5.Blend egg yolks and lemon juice until smooth.
  6. Step 6.With the blender on low, stream in the warm melted butter slowly until the sauce becomes thick and glossy.
  7. Step 7.Season with salt and cayenne, loosening with a little warm water only if the sauce is too thick.
  8. Step 8.Lift the eggs with a slotted spoon and blot the underside briefly.
  9. Step 9.Place one egg over each bacon-topped muffin and spoon the warm hollandaise over it.
  10. Step 10.Finish with chives and serve without delay.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Keep poaching water at a quiet simmer.
  • Add butter slowly so the hollandaise stays emulsified.
  • Have the muffins and bacon ready before poaching eggs.

! Common Mistakes

  • Rapidly boiling water can tear delicate whites.
  • Butter added too quickly can split hollandaise.
  • A finished Benedict cools quickly if it sits before serving.

↔ Substitutions

  • Ham or crisp bacon can replace Canadian bacon.
  • Toasted sourdough rounds can replace English muffins.
  • Add spinach for a Florentine-style variation.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Poached eggs are at their best immediately.
  • Refrigerate leftover cooked components separately for up to two days.
  • Make hollandaise fresh for the most reliable texture.
