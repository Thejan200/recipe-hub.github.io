Sticky Toffee Pudding

A tender date sponge with warm brown-sugar toffee sauce, made for serving while the sauce can soak into the cake.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Let the date mixture cool slightly before it meets the egg batter. Keep this in mind too: Large unsoftened date pieces create uneven texture.

Ingredients

  • 225 g pitted dates, chopped
  • 240 ml boiling water
  • 1 tsp baking soda
  • 75 g softened butter
  • 150 g dark brown sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 175 g self-raising flour
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 100 g butter for sauce
  • 150 g dark brown sugar for sauce
  • 180 ml double cream
  • 1 tbsp golden syrup

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Heat the oven to 180°C / 160°C fan and grease a deep baking dish. Put the dates in a heatproof bowl, cover with the boiling water, stir in the baking soda, and leave until the dates are very soft.
  2. Step 2.Blend or mash the date mixture until mostly smooth, leaving a little texture if you prefer. Set it aside to cool slightly so it does not scramble the eggs.
  3. Step 3.Beat the softened butter and brown sugar until combined and lighter in colour. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then mix in the vanilla.
  4. Step 4.Fold the flour and salt into the butter mixture just until no dry streaks remain. Fold in the date mixture with a spatula; stop as soon as the batter is even.
  5. Step 5.Pour into the prepared dish and bake until the sponge springs back lightly and a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs, about 30 to 35 minutes.
  6. Step 6.While the sponge bakes, place the sauce butter, brown sugar, cream and golden syrup in a small saucepan. Warm gently, stirring until smooth, then simmer briefly only until glossy.
  7. Step 7.Pierce the warm sponge in several places, spoon over some sauce, and rest it for 5 minutes. Serve slices with the remaining warm sauce.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Let the date mixture cool slightly before it meets the egg batter.
  • Fold flour only until incorporated to keep the sponge tender.
  • Keep the sauce at a gentle simmer rather than a rolling boil.

! Common Mistakes

  • Large unsoftened date pieces create uneven texture.
  • Overmixing after flour is added can make the sponge heavy.
  • Cooking the sauce too long can make the sugar seize or turn grainy.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use heavy cream where double cream is not available.
  • Use dark corn syrup instead of golden syrup if needed.
  • Bake the batter in individual dishes and begin checking them earlier.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate cooled sponge and sauce separately for up to 4 days.
  • Warm the sauce slowly and heat sponge portions until hot before serving.
  • Freeze the plain sponge, well wrapped, for up to 2 months.
