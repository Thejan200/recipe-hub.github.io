Bubble and Squeak

A skillet of crisp potato-and-cabbage cakes with a soft centre, designed to turn cold cooked vegetables into a satisfying British-style breakfast.

KITCHEN NOTE

Practical cooking note: Use thoroughly cold cooked potato so the patties are easier to shape. One thing to watch for is Loose, warm potato mixture spreads instead of forming cakes.

Ingredients

  • 500 g cooked potatoes, mashed or crushed
  • 250 g cooked cabbage, finely chopped
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 large egg, optional for binding

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Put the potatoes, cabbage, onion, parsley, salt and pepper in a bowl. Mix with a fork until the cabbage is spread evenly through the potato; beat in the egg only if the mixture will not hold when squeezed.
  2. Step 2.Divide the mixture into eight portions and press each into a compact patty about 2 cm thick. If the mixture feels sticky, chill the patties for 10 minutes while the pan heats.
  3. Step 3.Warm the butter and oil in a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Arrange the patties with space between them so steam can escape.
  4. Step 4.Cook without moving them until the base is deeply golden and releases easily from the pan, about 5 to 7 minutes.
  5. Step 5.Turn each patty carefully with a wide spatula and brown the second side for another 4 to 6 minutes. Reduce the heat if the butter darkens too quickly.
  6. Step 6.Transfer the cakes to a warm plate and let them stand for 2 minutes before serving; this helps the potato set and keeps the crust crisp.
  7. Step 7.Serve immediately with eggs, chutney, or a simple salad.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Use thoroughly cold cooked potato so the patties are easier to shape.
  • Finely chop and squeeze very wet cabbage before mixing it in.
  • Wait until the first side releases naturally before attempting to turn a cake.

! Common Mistakes

  • Loose, warm potato mixture spreads instead of forming cakes.
  • Too much moisture from cabbage makes the centre soggy.
  • Flipping before a crust forms breaks the patties.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use cooked Brussels sprouts or kale in place of cabbage.
  • A chopped spring onion can replace the small onion.
  • Skip the egg if firm leftover mash already binds well.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate cooled cakes for up to 3 days.
  • Reheat in a dry skillet or oven so the outside becomes crisp again.
  • Freeze cooked cakes in a single layer before packing them for up to 1 month.
