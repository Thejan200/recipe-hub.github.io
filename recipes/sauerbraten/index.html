German Sauerbraten

A vinegar-marinated German pot roast braised until tender with a balanced sweet-sour gravy.

KITCHEN NOTE

Kitchen note: Use a glass, ceramic, or stainless container for the acidic marinade. A common setback is Marinating in reactive aluminum can give the roast an unpleasant metallic flavor.

Ingredients

  • 3 lb beef chuck roast
  • 1 cup red wine vinegar
  • 2 cups beef stock
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 2 carrots, sliced
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 8 juniper berries
  • 6 whole cloves
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 3 gingersnap cookies, crushed
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • salt and pepper

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Combine vinegar, stock, onion, carrots, bay, juniper, and cloves in a nonreactive container, then cool the liquid completely before it touches the beef.
  2. Step 2.Submerge the roast in the cold marinade, cover it, and refrigerate for two to three days, turning it once daily so the seasoning reaches all sides evenly.
  3. Step 3.Lift out the roast and pat it dry, then strain and reserve the marinade; drying the surface helps the meat brown instead of steaming in the pot.
  4. Step 4.Heat oil in a Dutch oven and sear the beef on every side until it develops a dark crust, working patiently rather than trying to brown several wet surfaces at once.
  5. Step 5.Pour in enough strained marinade to come partway up the roast, cover tightly, and braise at a low oven temperature until a fork enters with little resistance.
  6. Step 6.Move the beef to a warm plate and strain the cooking liquid, then whisk flour with a little cool liquid before adding it back to prevent lumps.
  7. Step 7.Simmer the sauce with crushed gingersnaps and brown sugar until it becomes glossy and lightly thickened, tasting for a pleasant sweet-sour balance.
  8. Step 8.Slice the rested roast across the grain and spoon the gravy over it, serving with red cabbage and dumplings or potatoes.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Use a glass, ceramic, or stainless container for the acidic marinade.

! Common Mistakes

  • Marinating in reactive aluminum can give the roast an unpleasant metallic flavor.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use apple cider vinegar for a softer acidity if red wine vinegar is too sharp.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate covered leftovers for up to 4 days; the flavor develops well overnight.
