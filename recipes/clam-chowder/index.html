Clam Chowder

A New England-inspired clam chowder with tender potato, bacon, thyme, and clams folded into a creamy, savoury broth at the end of cooking.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: The liquid from canned clams adds flavour, so measure it into the pot along with the clam juice. To avoid a common problem, remember: Simmering clams for too long makes them firm and chewy.

Ingredients

  • 4 slices thick-cut bacon, diced
  • 1 medium yellow onion, diced
  • 2 celery ribs, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3 bottles (8 oz each) clam juice
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 1/2 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, diced
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 3 cans (6.5 oz each) chopped clams, drained with liquid reserved
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • Chopped parsley and oyster crackers, for serving

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Cook the diced bacon in a heavy pot over medium heat until it is crisp, then transfer it to a plate and leave about 2 tablespoons of fat in the pot.
  2. Step 2.Cook the onion and celery in the bacon fat until softened, then add garlic and stir just until its aroma blooms.
  3. Step 3.Mix in the flour and keep stirring for a minute so it coats the vegetables and loses its raw taste.
  4. Step 4.Whisk in clam juice, the reserved clam liquid, and chicken broth gradually until the base is smooth.
  5. Step 5.Add the potatoes, thyme, and bay leaf. Simmer partly covered until the potatoes are tender but still hold their cubes, about 15 to 18 minutes.
  6. Step 6.Turn the heat low and stir in the cream, warming gently without letting the chowder boil.
  7. Step 7.Add drained clams and most of the bacon, then heat only until the clams are warmed through, about 2 to 3 minutes.
  8. Step 8.Discard the bay leaf, taste before adding salt, and serve with parsley, remaining bacon, and oyster crackers.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • The liquid from canned clams adds flavour, so measure it into the pot along with the clam juice.
  • Wait until the potatoes are tender before adding clams, which need only brief warming.
  • Cut the potatoes to similar size so they finish at the same time.

! Common Mistakes

  • Simmering clams for too long makes them firm and chewy.
  • Boiling a cream-based chowder after the dairy is added can affect its texture.
  • Seasoning aggressively before tasting can make the chowder too salty because clam liquid varies.

↔ Substitutions

  • Russet potatoes give a softer texture and release more starch into the broth.
  • Additional clam juice may replace the chicken broth for a stronger seafood note.
  • Half-and-half can replace heavy cream for a lighter chowder.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate leftovers promptly in a covered container for up to 3 days.
  • Reheat over low heat until steaming, without bringing the chowder to a boil.
  • Freezing is not recommended because potato and cream textures can deteriorate after thawing.
