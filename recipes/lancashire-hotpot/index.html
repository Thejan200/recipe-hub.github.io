Lancashire Hotpot

Slow-baked Lancashire lamb and onions under overlapping crisp golden potato slices.

Published 2026-09-19

⏱ 160 min🍽 6 servings🔥 590 cal
DinnerUK

Ingredients

  • 2 lb lamb shoulder, cut into 1 1/2-inch pieces
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 2 large onions, sliced
  • 2 carrots, thickly sliced
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 cups lamb or beef stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 2 lb russet potatoes, peeled and thinly sliced
  • 3 tbsp melted butter

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Pat the lamb dry, season it with salt and pepper, dust with the measured flour, and shake away loose flour that could burn during browning.
  2. Step 2.Heat oil in a large ovenproof casserole and brown the lamb in small batches, developing color on every side before transferring it to a clean plate.
  3. Step 3.Cook the onions and carrots in the same casserole until lightly golden, scraping the base so the browned meat residue flavors the vegetables.
  4. Step 4.Return the lamb, add Worcestershire sauce, stock, bay leaves, and thyme, and bring the liquid just to a simmer before turning off the heat.
  5. Step 5.Arrange the thin potato slices in tightly overlapping circles across the entire surface, creating an even lid that protects the meat as it slowly braises.
  6. Step 6.Brush the potatoes with melted butter, cover the casserole, and bake at 325°F for ninety minutes until the lamb is tender beneath the potato layer.
  7. Step 7.Remove the lid, increase the oven to 400°F, and bake another 30 to 40 minutes until the exposed potato edges are crisp and richly golden.
  8. Step 8.Rest the hotpot for ten minutes, remove the bay leaves when encountered, and serve by lifting through both crisp potato and tender lamb layers.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Lamb shoulder becomes tender during slow baking.
  • Slice potatoes evenly for uniform cooking.
  • Uncover only for the final browning stage.

! Common Mistakes

  • Lean lamb cuts can dry during the long bake.
  • Thick potato slices may remain firm.
  • Too much stock prevents the potato base from setting.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use lamb neck fillet instead of shoulder.
  • Use beef stock when lamb stock is unavailable.
  • Use Maris Piper potatoes in place of russets.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate covered for up to 3 days.
  • Reheat uncovered at 350°F until 165°F.
  • Freeze cooled portions for up to 2 months.
