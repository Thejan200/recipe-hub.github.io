Scones

Tall, tender English scones with flaky layers, ready for jam and clotted cream.

KITCHEN NOTE

Practical cooking note: Keep butter cold and handle lightly. One thing to watch for is Kneading heavily makes scones tough.

Ingredients

  • 500 g plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  • 2 rounded tsp baking powder
  • 2 heaped tbsp caster sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 110 g cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 300 ml whole milk
  • 1 large egg, beaten, for egg wash
  • Clotted cream and jam, optional for serving

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F), line a baking tray, and chill the cubed butter until the moment it is needed.
  2. Step 2.Mix the flour, baking powder, caster sugar, and salt thoroughly so the raising agent is distributed through the large quantity of flour.
  3. Step 3.Rub the cold butter into the dry ingredients until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with a few small flakes still visible.
  4. Step 4.Pour in the whole milk and mix gently with a table knife or hand just until a soft, slightly rough dough comes together.
  5. Step 5.Turn onto a lightly floured surface, fold the dough over itself a few times for layers, and pat it out without heavy kneading.
  6. Step 6.Cut straight down with a round cutter, avoiding a twisting motion, and gather scraps gently to cut the remaining scones.
  7. Step 7.Arrange on the lined tray, brush only the tops with beaten egg, and keep egg wash away from the sides so they can rise freely.
  8. Step 8.Bake for 15 to 20 minutes until tall and golden, then cool briefly on a rack and serve warm with optional jam and clotted cream.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Keep butter cold and handle lightly.
  • Cut straight down without twisting.
  • Place scones close together for straighter sides.

! Common Mistakes

  • Kneading heavily makes scones tough.
  • Twisting the cutter seals the edges.
  • Egg wash down the sides restricts rise.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use unsalted block butter rather than spreadable butter.
  • Use full-fat dairy milk for the demonstrated texture.
  • Serve plain when cream and jam are not desired.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Best eaten the day they are baked.
  • Keep airtight for up to 2 days and warm before serving.
  • Freeze cooled scones for up to 2 months.
