Pulled Pork

A slow-cooker pork shoulder recipe that finishes with distinct, juicy shreds in a tangy barbecue cooking sauce for sandwiches or plated meals.

Ingredients

  • 4 lb boneless pork shoulder, trimmed of excess surface fat
  • 1 large yellow onion, sliced
  • 1 tbsp kosher salt
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken broth
  • 1 cup barbecue sauce, plus more for serving
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 8 soft sandwich buns

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Dry the pork shoulder and remove only loose, heavy surface fat; the marbling within the cut is useful during a long, gentle cook.
  2. Step 2.Stir the salt, paprika, pepper, and garlic powder together, then massage the mixture over the entire shoulder.
  3. Step 3.Put the sliced onion in the cooker as a base and settle the seasoned pork on top.
  4. Step 4.Mix the broth, barbecue sauce, Worcestershire sauce, brown sugar, and cider vinegar. Pour this around the pork, leaving some of its seasoned surface uncovered.
  5. Step 5.Cook covered on LOW until a fork enters with very little resistance and a small piece separates easily; the stated time is a guide, not the only test.
  6. Step 6.Move the pork to a tray. Spoon off any excess fat from the cooker liquid before using it for the finished meat.
  7. Step 7.Pull the shoulder apart while it is still warm, removing any tough connective pieces and keeping some larger bites among the fine shreds.
  8. Step 8.Return the pork to the cooker and add cooking liquid a little at a time, stopping when the meat is moist rather than swimming in sauce.
  9. Step 9.Taste a small portion and balance it with barbecue sauce, vinegar, salt, or pepper only if needed.
  10. Step 10.Serve warm in toasted buns, or use the pork as a filling for baked potatoes, rice bowls, or tacos.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • A shoulder is ready when it separates under a fork; an elapsed time alone is not enough.
  • Reserve the defatted cooking liquid so each serving can be moistened as needed.
  • Toast sandwich buns shortly before serving so they stay sturdy.

! Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a very lean pork cut leaves little protection against drying during a long cook.
  • Checking repeatedly releases heat and can lengthen the cook.
  • Mixing all of the liquid back at once can dilute the pork's texture.

↔ Substitutions

  • A bone-in shoulder can be used; remove the bone after it releases easily.
  • Apple juice can replace broth for a fruitier, sweeter cooking liquid.
  • Skip the buns and serve the finished pork with grains or roasted potatoes.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Chill leftovers with a little sauce or cooking liquid for up to four days.
  • Warm small portions gently with an added spoonful of liquid if they look dry.
  • Freeze cooled portions in sealed containers for up to three months.
