Ploughman's Lunch

A British cold lunch board centred on bread, cheese, and pickled onions, with optional ham and seasonal accompaniments.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: This is a flexible board, so choose components that suit the people being served. Keep this in mind too: Without something pickled, the board can lack the sharp contrast that balances rich cheese.

Ingredients

  • Good bread
  • A selection of local cheeses
  • Pickled onions
  • Optional baked ham
  • Optional locally sourced pickles or seasonal produce

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Choose bread, one or more cheeses, and pickled onions as the base of the board.
  2. Step 2.Add baked ham only if wanted, together with any seasonal fruit, salad items, or additional pickles you enjoy.
  3. Step 3.Place moist items in separate small dishes or at the edge of the platter so the bread stays dry.
  4. Step 4.Serve at once as a cold, build-your-own lunch.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • This is a flexible board, so choose components that suit the people being served.
  • Keep bread, cheese, and pickled onions present for the core contrast of the plate.
  • Keep wet pickles away from bread until the moment of eating.

! Common Mistakes

  • Without something pickled, the board can lack the sharp contrast that balances rich cheese.
  • Trying to make every optional addition mandatory defeats the flexible format.
  • Placing wet pickles on bread too early makes it soggy.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use any cheese combination that offers different textures or strengths.
  • Leave out ham for a meat-free board.
  • Other sharp pickles can accompany or replace pickled onions if needed.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Assemble the board immediately before serving.
  • Refrigerate perishable components separately for up to two days.
  • If serving cut apple, coat it with lemon juice to slow browning.
