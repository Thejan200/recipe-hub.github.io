Bread Pudding

A baked British bread pudding with softened stale bread, raisins, citrus, warming spice and a crisp demerara finish.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Give the bread enough soaking time to make the texture even. To avoid a common problem, remember: Dry bread pieces remain chewy after baking.

Ingredients

  • 300 g stale bread, torn
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 100 g raisins
  • 75 g mixed peel
  • 100 g brown sugar
  • 50 g melted butter
  • 1 large egg, beaten
  • 1 tsp mixed spice
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 2 tbsp demerara sugar

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Heat the oven to 170°C / 150°C fan and grease a square baking dish. Put the torn bread in a large bowl and pour over the milk, pressing the pieces down so they absorb it evenly.
  2. Step 2.Leave the bread to soften for about 20 minutes, then break it apart with a fork until no firm dry pieces remain.
  3. Step 3.Stir in the raisins, mixed peel, brown sugar, melted butter, egg, mixed spice, cinnamon and lemon zest until the fruit and seasoning are evenly distributed.
  4. Step 4.Spoon the mixture into the prepared dish and level the surface. Sprinkle the demerara sugar over the top for a lightly crisp finish.
  5. Step 5.Bake until the centre feels set and the top is browned, about 65 to 75 minutes. A skewer should come out without wet batter, though the pudding will remain moist.
  6. Step 6.Leave the dish to cool for at least 20 minutes before cutting; the pudding firms as it rests.
  7. Step 7.Serve warm or at room temperature, cut into squares.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Give the bread enough soaking time to make the texture even.
  • Use a fork rather than a blender so the pudding keeps some body.
  • Resting before slicing produces cleaner portions.

! Common Mistakes

  • Dry bread pieces remain chewy after baking.
  • Overworking the soaked bread makes the finished pudding dense.
  • Cutting straight from the oven causes the soft centre to crumble.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use sultanas or chopped dried apricots instead of raisins.
  • Swap orange zest for lemon zest.
  • Use light brown sugar if dark brown sugar is unavailable.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Store cooled pudding airtight in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.
  • Warm individual portions gently in the oven or microwave.
  • Freeze wrapped squares for up to 2 months.
