Lamb Curry

Slow-cooked lamb shoulder in a warmly spiced tomato and yogurt sauce, finished with fresh coriander for a rich weeknight curry.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Brown the lamb in small batches so it sears instead of steaming. To avoid a common problem, remember: Putting all of the lamb in the pot at once prevents proper browning.

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil
  • 900 g boneless lamb shoulder, trimmed and cut into 3 cm pieces
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 tbsp mild curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp chilli powder
  • 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
  • 240 ml lamb or beef stock
  • 120 g plain yogurt
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh coriander

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Pat the lamb dry and season it with the salt and pepper. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat, then brown the lamb in batches; transfer it to a plate once it has colour on several sides.
  2. Step 2.Lower the heat to medium and cook the onion in the same pot until soft and lightly golden, scraping up the browned lamb juices as it releases moisture.
  3. Step 3.Stir in the garlic and ginger for about 30 seconds, then add the curry powder, cumin, coriander, turmeric and chilli powder. Keep the spices moving just until fragrant so they do not catch.
  4. Step 4.Return the lamb to the pot and add the tomatoes and stock. Bring the sauce to a gentle bubble, cover, and cook on low heat until the lamb is tender enough to pull apart with a fork, about 70 to 80 minutes.
  5. Step 5.Uncover for the final 10 minutes if the sauce needs reducing. Stir occasionally and add a small splash of stock if it begins to stick before the lamb is tender.
  6. Step 6.Take the pot off the heat and stir in the yogurt a spoonful at a time. This keeps the sauce smooth; do not return it to a hard boil after adding the yogurt.
  7. Step 7.Check the seasoning, scatter over coriander, and serve the curry hot with rice, flatbread, or both.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Brown the lamb in small batches so it sears instead of steaming.
  • Keep the simmer quiet; shoulder becomes tender through slow, moist cooking.
  • Let the pot sit for a few minutes before serving so the sauce settles.

! Common Mistakes

  • Putting all of the lamb in the pot at once prevents proper browning.
  • High heat after yogurt is added can make the sauce split.
  • Stopping the cook too early leaves shoulder chewy rather than tender.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use beef chuck cut into cubes when lamb is unavailable.
  • Choose plain full-fat Greek yogurt for a thicker finish.
  • Leave out the chilli powder for a gentler curry.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Cool leftovers promptly and refrigerate them in a covered container for up to 3 days.
  • Freeze cooled portions for up to 3 months.
  • Reheat slowly until the centre is steaming hot.
