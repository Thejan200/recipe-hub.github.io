Pork Carnitas

Citrus-and-spice braised pork shoulder, pulled into varied pieces and finished under high heat for crisp edges and tender centres.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Shoulder has the fat and connective tissue that makes this long-cooked style work. To avoid a common problem, remember: Lean cuts can dry out before they become tender.

Ingredients

  • 4 lb boneless pork shoulder, cut into 2-inch chunks
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 5 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 1 orange, juiced
  • 1 lime, juiced
  • 1 small onion, quartered
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 tbsp lard or neutral oil

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Set the oven to 300°F. Cut the shoulder into similar-sized chunks, leaving enough attached fat to protect the meat through the long braise.
  2. Step 2.Season the pork with salt, pepper, cumin, oregano, and cinnamon, turning it until the spices are distributed rather than sitting in clumps.
  3. Step 3.Place the pork in a Dutch oven with garlic, citrus juices, onion, bay leaves, and lard or oil.
  4. Step 4.Cover and roast until the pork has begun to soften, turning the pieces once midway so they benefit from the cooking juices.
  5. Step 5.Remove the lid and continue cooking until a fork readily breaks a piece apart; timing may vary with the cut and pan.
  6. Step 6.Lift the pork out. Strain the liquid, discard the spent onion and bay leaves, and keep the flavourful juices and rendered fat.
  7. Step 7.Break the meat into a mix of bite-sized pieces and shreds rather than reducing it to a uniform pile.
  8. Step 8.Spread it over a rimmed tray and drizzle with a few spoonfuls of the reserved liquid.
  9. Step 9.Broil briefly, toss, then broil again until the outer edges are browned and crisp while the interior stays succulent.
  10. Step 10.Season after crisping if needed, and serve immediately in tortillas, bowls, or plates with fresh toppings.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Shoulder has the fat and connective tissue that makes this long-cooked style work.
  • Save the cooking liquid before crisping; it restores moisture without making the pork heavy.
  • Use a broad, uncrowded tray so the final high heat browns rather than steams.

! Common Mistakes

  • Lean cuts can dry out before they become tender.
  • Very fine shreds can turn hard during the final crisping step.
  • Discarding the juices removes the easiest way to season and moisten the pork.

↔ Substitutions

  • A grapefruit-and-lime combination can stand in for orange and lime.
  • Neutral oil can replace lard.
  • A hot skillet can crisp small portions when a broiler is unavailable.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate cooled carnitas with a little reserved liquid for up to four days.
  • Re-crisp a portion in a hot skillet before serving.
  • Freeze pork and its cooking juices in sealed portions for up to three months.
