German Schweinshaxe

A Bavarian-style roasted pork knuckle with crisp skin, tender meat, and a simple beer pan gravy.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: A dry rind and high final heat are the keys to crisp crackling. To avoid a common problem, remember: Basting the skin repeatedly makes it soft instead of blistered.

Ingredients

  • 2 pork knuckles
  • 2 teaspoons caraway seeds
  • 1 teaspoon fennel seeds
  • 2 teaspoons salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 2 carrots, chunked
  • 12 ounces dark beer
  • 1 cup stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons cold water

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Pat the pork knuckles thoroughly dry and score the skin in shallow lines, keeping the cuts close enough to help fat render but not so deep that the meat dries.
  2. Step 2.Crush caraway, fennel, salt, and pepper, then rub the mixture over the knuckles and into the scored skin for an even aromatic crust.
  3. Step 3.Scatter onion, carrots, and bay leaves in a roasting pan, set the pork on top, and pour beer and stock around rather than over the skin.
  4. Step 4.Roast at a moderate heat until the meat begins to soften, basting only the exposed meat if needed while leaving the skin as dry as possible.
  5. Step 5.Increase the oven temperature for the last stage and roast until the rind turns blistered and crisp, rotating the pan if one side colors faster.
  6. Step 6.Rest the knuckles loosely tented while straining the pan liquid into a saucepan, then skim excess fat before making the gravy.
  7. Step 7.Thicken the simmering liquid with a cold cornstarch slurry, stirring until it is smooth and lightly coats the back of a spoon.
  8. Step 8.Serve one pork knuckle per plate with sauerkraut, potato dumplings, and the warm beer gravy.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • A dry rind and high final heat are the keys to crisp crackling.

! Common Mistakes

  • Basting the skin repeatedly makes it soft instead of blistered.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use a dark nonalcoholic beer or extra stock in place of beer.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Remove meat from the bone and refrigerate for up to 3 days; re-crisp skin in a hot oven.
