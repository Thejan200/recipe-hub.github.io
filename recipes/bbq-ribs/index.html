BBQ Ribs

Spare ribs cooked over indirect heat with a paprika-forward rub, then tenderized in a covered finish and lightly glazed just before serving.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Keep the ribs away from direct flame because the rub and sauce contain sugar. To avoid a common problem, remember: An intact membrane can leave the underside unpleasantly chewy.

Ingredients

  • 2 racks pork spare ribs, about 5 lb total
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1 cup barbecue sauce
  • 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Lift one edge of the membrane on the bone side of each rack and pull it away; a paper towel makes the slippery layer easier to grip.
  2. Step 2.Dry the ribs. Coat them thinly with mustard, then combine the sugar, paprika, salt, garlic powder, onion powder, pepper, and cayenne for the rub.
  3. Step 3.Press the rub onto both sides while the smoker or covered grill comes to a steady 250°F indirect zone.
  4. Step 4.Set the ribs bone-side down away from direct fire. Cook with the lid closed until the seasoning has darkened and feels set on the surface.
  5. Step 5.Place each rack on foil with butter and a small splash of cider vinegar. Seal the foil well so the ribs can gently braise in their own juices.
  6. Step 6.Return the parcels to indirect heat. Near the end, open one carefully and test the meat between the bones for tenderness.
  7. Step 7.Unwrap the racks and put them back bone-side down. Brush the meat surface with a restrained layer of barbecue sauce.
  8. Step 8.Continue cooking uncovered until the glaze looks tacky rather than wet; add one more thin coat only if the first has set.
  9. Step 9.Lift a rack with tongs: it should flex and show surface cracking, yet not disintegrate or lose the meat from every bone.
  10. Step 10.Let the racks stand briefly, then cut between the bones and serve with warmed sauce if wanted.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Keep the ribs away from direct flame because the rub and sauce contain sugar.
  • Build the glaze in thin coats so it sets instead of becoming burnt or heavy.
  • Use bend and tenderness checks alongside the cooking time.

! Common Mistakes

  • An intact membrane can leave the underside unpleasantly chewy.
  • Dense, prolonged smoke can overpower the pork and taste bitter.
  • Adding sauce too early risks blackened sugar before the ribs are tender.

↔ Substitutions

  • St. Louis-cut racks are a good alternative when a more even shape is useful.
  • A trusted commercial rub can replace the listed dry seasoning.
  • An oven held at 250°F can finish the covered stage if outdoor cooking must stop.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate cooked ribs for up to four days.
  • Reheat portions covered in foil at 275°F until hot through.
  • Freeze tightly wrapped cooled ribs for up to two months.
