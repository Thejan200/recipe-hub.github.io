Baby Back Ribs

Oven-baked baby back ribs with a balanced smoky seasoning, a moist covered bake, and a short high-heat barbecue finish.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Removing the membrane gives both the seasoning and bite a more even result. Keep this in mind too: Cooking until every bone is loose can make the rack soft and hard to slice.

Ingredients

  • 2 racks baby back ribs, about 4 lb total
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 cup barbecue sauce
  • 2 tbsp apple cider juice or water

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Set the oven to 300°F and cover a rimmed tray with foil. A rack is optional, but the tray needs high sides to contain the cooking juices.
  2. Step 2.Remove the membrane from the back of the baby back racks, then dry the meat before seasoning.
  3. Step 3.Blend salt, paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, pepper, cumin, and brown sugar. Work this mixture over both sides of the ribs.
  4. Step 4.Arrange the racks on the tray and add the apple juice or water to the tray rather than on top of the seasoning. Cover the tray very tightly with foil.
  5. Step 5.Bake until the meat near the ends of the bones has drawn back a little and the racks bend more readily when supported with tongs.
  6. Step 6.If the meat still feels firm, reseal the tray and continue baking in short intervals instead of relying on the clock alone.
  7. Step 7.Take away the foil, raise the oven to 425°F, and brush the ribs with a light coat of barbecue sauce.
  8. Step 8.Bake uncovered until the sauce bubbles and clings to the meat. Keep watching after it starts to darken, as sugary sauce changes quickly at this temperature.
  9. Step 9.For deeper colour, add a final thin brushing and broil only briefly under close supervision.
  10. Step 10.Rest the racks for about 10 minutes, then slice cleanly and serve while the glaze is still warm.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Removing the membrane gives both the seasoning and bite a more even result.
  • A well-sealed foil cover keeps the gentle baking stage moist.
  • Treat the high-heat glaze as a short finishing step, not a second long cook.

! Common Mistakes

  • Cooking until every bone is loose can make the rack soft and hard to slice.
  • Foil gaps allow steam to escape and slow the tenderizing stage.
  • A thick layer of sauce can scorch before it turns glossy.

↔ Substitutions

  • Water, broth, or apple juice all work as the small amount of tray liquid.
  • Choose a hot barbecue sauce when a spicier finish is desired.
  • A grill can replace the final oven glaze if it is monitored closely.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Keep cooled sliced ribs sealed in the refrigerator for up to four days.
  • Rewarm covered at 300°F until hot through.
  • Freeze wrapped cooked ribs for up to two months.
