Lamb Shanks

Slow-braised lamb shanks in a rich tomato and red-wine gravy for a comforting Australian-style dinner.

KITCHEN NOTE

Cook’s focus: Braise gently; a hard boil can make lamb shanks dry rather than tender. Keep this in mind too: Skipping the browning step, which leaves the sauce flat in flavour.

Ingredients

  • 4 lamb shanks
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 cup red wine
  • 2 cups beef stock
  • 400 g crushed tomatoes
  • rosemary and thyme

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Season the lamb shanks generously and brown them in olive oil in a heavy oven-safe pot until every side has a deep colour.
  2. Step 2.Remove the shanks and cook onion and carrots in the same pot, scraping up the browned flavourful bits left on the base.
  3. Step 3.Add garlic and tomato paste, stirring briefly until fragrant and darkened slightly without allowing the garlic to burn.
  4. Step 4.Pour in red wine and simmer it down, using a wooden spoon to release the caramelised pieces from the bottom of the pot.
  5. Step 5.Add stock, tomatoes and herbs, then return the lamb shanks to the pot with liquid coming roughly halfway up their sides.
  6. Step 6.Cover tightly and braise in a low oven until the meat is very tender and beginning to pull away from the bone.
  7. Step 7.Lift out the shanks carefully and simmer the sauce uncovered if needed until it is glossy enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  8. Step 8.Serve each shank over creamy mash or polenta with vegetables, spooning the hot reduced gravy generously over the meat.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Braise gently; a hard boil can make lamb shanks dry rather than tender.

! Common Mistakes

  • Skipping the browning step, which leaves the sauce flat in flavour.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use alcohol-free stock in place of wine and add a little extra tomato paste.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate for three days; the flavour improves after an overnight rest.
