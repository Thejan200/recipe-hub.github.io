Pecan Pie

A pecan-studded holiday pie with a buttery pastry base and a rich, set syrup filling that holds together for serving.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Taste a pecan before starting; fresh nuts should taste sweet and buttery rather than harsh or stale. To avoid a common problem, remember: Cutting the pie before it has cooled long enough leaves the filling too soft to hold a neat slice.

Ingredients

  • 1 unbaked 9-inch pie crust
  • 1 1/2 cups pecan halves
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 cup light corn syrup
  • 3/4 cup packed brown sugar
  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted and cooled slightly
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon bourbon, optional

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Heat the oven to 350°F. Fit the pastry into a 9-inch pie plate, shape the edge, and keep it cold while you assemble the filling.
  2. Step 2.Scatter the pecans across the chilled shell. Reserve a few attractive halves for the surface if you would like a more defined top after baking.
  3. Step 3.In a separate bowl, whisk the eggs until blended. Stir in the corn syrup, brown sugar, melted butter, vanilla, salt, and bourbon if using until the sugar begins to dissolve.
  4. Step 4.Pour the liquid carefully over the nuts. Nudge any floating pecans into an even layer without pressing them deeply into the crust.
  5. Step 5.Put the pie plate on a rimmed baking sheet and bake for 45 to 55 minutes. It is ready when the outer filling is puffed and stable while the very centre still gives a restrained tremble.
  6. Step 6.If the pastry edge reaches a deep golden colour before the filling is done, tent just the rim with foil and return the pie to the oven.
  7. Step 7.Cool the pie on a rack for at least 3 hours before slicing so the filling can set instead of flowing at the cut edge.
  8. Step 8.Use a sharp knife for portions, wiping the blade between cuts. Serve as it is or with lightly sweetened cream.

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Taste a pecan before starting; fresh nuts should taste sweet and buttery rather than harsh or stale.
  • Place the pie plate on the baking tray before pouring in the filling, not after, to make transport safer.
  • A small movement in the centre is expected at the end of baking, but the filling should not look loose or sloshy.

! Common Mistakes

  • Cutting the pie before it has cooled long enough leaves the filling too soft to hold a neat slice.
  • Allowing the pastry edge to overbrown while waiting for the centre to finish can make the crust bitter.
  • Adding heavily salted pecans without reducing the added salt can unbalance the sweet filling.

↔ Substitutions

  • Use dark corn syrup for a deeper flavour and darker finish.
  • Leave out the bourbon or replace it with an equal amount of milk.
  • Maple syrup can replace part of the corn syrup, although the finished filling will be softer and more maple-forward.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Let the pie reach room temperature before covering it.
  • Store leftovers in the refrigerator for up to 4 days.
  • Freeze tightly wrapped individual portions for up to 2 months and thaw them overnight in the refrigerator.
