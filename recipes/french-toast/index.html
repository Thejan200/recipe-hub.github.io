French Toast

Skillet French toast made with thick bread, a cinnamon-vanilla egg custard, and a gently browned buttery finish for breakfast or brunch.

KITCHEN NOTE

Before you begin: Thick, slightly stale bread holds its shape better than soft fresh slices. To avoid a common problem, remember: Thin, fresh bread can collapse if it stays in the custard too long.

Ingredients

  • 8 thick slices brioche or challah
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter
  • Maple syrup and berries, for serving

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Leave the bread uncovered briefly so its surface is dry enough to take up custard without breaking down.
  2. Step 2.Whisk the eggs, milk, vanilla, cinnamon, sugar, and salt in a shallow dish until the egg is fully dispersed.
  3. Step 3.Heat a wide skillet or griddle over medium-low heat; the aim is to set the custard before the bread becomes too dark.
  4. Step 4.Melt a little butter and coat the cooking surface.
  5. Step 5.Soak two slices at a time, turning once, until the custard has reached the middle without saturating the bread.
  6. Step 6.Lift each slice and allow excess custard to fall back into the dish.
  7. Step 7.Cook until the first side is golden and the edges look set.
  8. Step 8.Turn and brown the second side, lowering the heat if the butter begins to darken too quickly.
  9. Step 9.Continue with the remaining bread, adding butter as needed and holding finished slices warm only briefly.
  10. Step 10.Serve hot with maple syrup, fruit, or a light dusting of cinnamon or icing sugar.

HELPFUL NOTES

Tips, Substitutions & Storage

Extra guidance to help you get a reliable result, avoid common problems, and know what to do with leftovers.

✓ Recipe Tips

  • Thick, slightly stale bread holds its shape better than soft fresh slices.
  • Moderate heat gives the egg mixture time to cook through.
  • Whisk the custard again between batches because cinnamon settles quickly.

! Common Mistakes

  • Thin, fresh bread can collapse if it stays in the custard too long.
  • High heat can brown the surface before the egg custard has set.
  • An overcrowded pan makes turning the slices difficult.

↔ Substitutions

  • Texas toast is a sturdy alternative to brioche or challah.
  • Replace part of the milk with half-and-half for a richer custard.
  • Dairy-free milk and plant-based butter can be used if needed.

⌂ Storage & Reheating

  • Refrigerate fully cooled slices for up to three days.
  • Reheat in a skillet or toaster oven to restore a crisp edge.
  • Freeze portions between parchment for up to two months.
