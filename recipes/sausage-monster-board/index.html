Sausage Monster Charcuterie Board

A playful Halloween charcuterie board with a sausage-and-cheese monster face, crisp crackers and fresh produce for contrast.

KITCHEN NOTE

Practical cooking note: Place the face features before filling the board's outer edges. One thing to watch for is Warm sausage slices can soften nearby crackers.

Published 2026-10-29

⏱ 25 min🍽 10 servings🔥 245 cal

↓ Jump to recipe

Ingredients

  • 250 g cooked cocktail sausages, sliced
  • 200 g cheddar cubes
  • 150 g mozzarella pearls
  • 2 cups crackers
  • 1 cucumber, sliced
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes
  • 2 large olives
  • 2 cheese rounds

Instructions

Follow each step in order. The directions below are written so a beginner can understand what to do, what to look for, and when to move on to the next step.

  1. Step 1.Set a wide board on a stable surface and gather all the components first, separating crisp crackers from ingredients that can release moisture.
  2. Step 2.Arrange sliced sausages in a rounded face shape, leaving a clear space near the top for eyes and along the bottom for a simple cheese toothy smile.
  3. Step 3.Place two cheese rounds near the top of the sausage circle and centre olive slices on them so the monster has bold, easy-to-recognise eyes.
  4. Step 4.Line the lower edge with small cheddar triangles or cubes to suggest teeth while keeping the pieces easy for guests to pick up.
  5. Step 5.Create colourful hair around the top with red pepper strips, then use cucumber rounds and cherry tomatoes to widen the board without hiding the face.
  6. Step 6.Fill remaining spaces with crackers, keeping at least one cracker group away from moist vegetables so it stays crisp throughout the party.
  7. Step 7.Add mozzarella pearls and extra olives in little clusters for variety rather than scattering them randomly across the monster outline.
  8. Step 8.Serve immediately or cover loosely and chill for a short time, bringing the board out before crackers lose their crunch.

! Common Mistakes

↔ Substitutions
